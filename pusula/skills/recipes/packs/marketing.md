# Pack: Marketing (store pages, content, email, search)

Read when the task writes or reviews marketing material: a store page, a launch plan, posts, emails, a landing page. Facts read 2026-10-25 on the pages named. Labels: [official], [reasoning], [unverified]. Sending, posting or publishing anything is an outward action: draft it, then wait for the user's explicit yes (AGENTS.md).

## Sources first
- Steam store page: https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/page and the Steamworks pages it links (graphical assets, trailers). Marketing hub: https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/marketing (index only; it names wishlists, update visibility rounds, announcements, Next Fest, discounts). [official]
- FTC endorsements: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers. Email: https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/can-spam-act-compliance-guide-business. Search: https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials. [official]
- AI-made content in a store page: Steam asks for AI disclosure (see `packs/ai-production.md`).

## Steam page rules (as read)
- Sections marked with (*) must be filled before review. Asset sizes and trailer specs live on the linked pages, not on this one: read them, do not guess sizes. [official]
- Description tab: no links to other websites (use the social and website fields); no imagery that imitates Steam UI such as wishlist buttons, capsules or prices; no images, links or widgets promoting other Steam games. [official]
- Publishing sends all page data, including unannounced content, to Steam's public servers. Preview with Store Beta Mode. After release, "Save" keeps changes private; publishing makes them live at once. The game name cannot change after pre-release review. [official]
- Next Fest has its own pages (event dates listed there: October 2026, February 2027, June 2027). [official]

## Disclosure and consent
- Endorsement or influencer posts: disclose every material connection, including free products; plain words like "ad" or "sponsored", not "sp", "collab" or a lone "thanks"; put it with the endorsement, inside the video itself (spoken or on screen), repeated in live streams. [official]
- Marketing email: accurate sender and subject, say it is an ad, give a postal address, a clear opt-out honored within 10 business days, and you stay responsible when an agency sends it. Penalty up to $53,088 per email as the page says. This is US law; EU and Turkish rules differ and were not read: [unverified], ask a lawyer. [official]
- Search: write for people, use the words the audience searches in titles, headings, alt text and link text, keep links crawlable. Meeting the page does not guarantee indexing. [official]

## Traps
1. Inventing numbers (wishlist targets, conversion rates, "best posting time"). Say "no source" or cite one. [reasoning]
2. Copying a competitor's trailer or key art structure too closely, or using Steam UI imagery. [official for the UI rule]
3. Claims in the page text that the build cannot prove (features not in the game). Check each claim against a screenshot or the build. [reasoning]
4. Posting from the user's accounts, sending email, or publishing a store page without a named yes. [AGENTS.md]

## Checks
- Every claim in the copy has a source line in the draft (store page, build, screenshot) or is removed.
- Steam text has no outside links and no Steam-UI imagery; email draft has all eight elements above; sponsored posts show the disclosure in the first line.
- Plain-language read-through (`plain-explain`) and a spell check in the target language.

## Done when
A draft file exists with sources per claim, the disclosure and consent items are checked, and nothing was sent or published.
